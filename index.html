💡Problem & Contributions

🚫 Problem
  • Glass creates two mapping failures: missed panes leave dangerous gaps, while misplaced reconstructions create phantom walls that block valid paths. Reliable navigation needs both glass coverage and clean free space.
✅ Contributions
  • Geometry verifies placement. Glass Killer constructs metric plane hypotheses from structural LiDAR cues, then cross-checks their orientation using depth-free image geometry and bounds their extent using the glass silhouette.
  • The map remains revisable. Multi-view reprojection and floor-consistency checks refine or remove hypotheses contradicted by later observations, keeping the planner’s glass map up to date.
  • Building-scale evaluation. Across nine scenes in six environments and 2.1 km of autonomous travel, Glass Killer achieves 80% total coverage versus at most 65% for the baselines under identical pinhole inputs, with 2–5× lower sustained spill and 4–7× fewer concurrent false voxels. The 360° variant detects 46.1% of eligible glass voxels at least 10 m away.

⚙️Method

Candidate glass is segmented in RGB; sparse LiDAR structure around the mask anchors a metric plane hypothesis; depth-free projective geometry verifies it before acceptance; the global map keeps every accepted plane revocable, and the planner consumes verified occupancy only.

Why the baselines struggle

MonoGlass3D attempts to solve a fundamentally metric problem with RGB alone. Its training corpus covers few scenes, so depth regression at range degrades quickly — panes are detected in the image but projected meters off the true surface, and every misprojection becomes standing phantom occupancy because nothing ever deletes it. The robot carries a LiDAR whose returns would anchor the scale directly, but the method never consumes them — they use RGB to solve a LiDAR problem.

GlassRecon never measures the pane: its glass surface is read directly from the Depth-Anything prior's output inside the glass mask (sensor returns on glass are deliberately discarded). This leans on a behavior that is a training artifact rather than a model ability — the foundation model sometimes renders glass as a solid wall and sometimes sees straight through it, depending on reflections and context. When the artifact cooperates, the aligned depth looks plausible; when it does not, whole panes are reconstructed at through-glass depth — a failure mode common to depth-prior pipelines on specular surfaces. Its RANSAC scale-shift alignment also costs seconds of CPU per frame (~0.2 Hz — one input every ~5 s in our runs), far below what a robot moving through a building can act on.

The hyperparameters that matter

KnobDefaultWhat it does / when to touch it
RANGE_M10One knob for sensing range: LiDAR crop, plane placement and spill radii, plane-fit ceiling. Raise (15–20) for atriums and outdoor facades.
CONF_TH0.3Segmentation confidence. Lower finds fainter glass but leans harder on the geometric verification to reject the extras.
--pinhole-dir-trust-min-span-px140Angle-gate arming: minimum edge span before the projective orientation check is trusted. Higher = gate arms less often (falls back to the conservative dv/dh test).
spill_thresh0.25Fraction of a plane seen “through” by later LiDAR before multi-view eviction fires. Lower = more aggressive self-correction.
min_cov0.10Minimum LiDAR seed coverage to place a plane at all — the floor on how little structural evidence is acceptable.
GROUNDING_CELL6 pxContact-seed grid resolution around the mask. Finer = tighter pane footprints, slightly more compute.
PIPELINEtrue2-process split: background mapping + foreground perception (the evaluated configuration). Set false for a simpler single-process run.
provider voxelSize0.05 mLiDAR stack voxel. 0.02 sharpens contacts but ~6× the CPU on large outdoor stacks.

Everything else ships frozen at the evaluated configuration — see TUNABLES.md in the code release.
